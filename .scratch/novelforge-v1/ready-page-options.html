NovelForge Ready Page

The page the player sees once their world and PC are made. All three keep your order: cover art, title, brief, then PC art. They use The Tide Comes In and Cass Whitlock, and each mockup follows your light or dark setting.

I recommend A. It's the closest to a presentation, and each piece gets the whole screen. You can pick one or mix parts.

A. Turn the pages

One piece at a time, moved along with a button.

Each piece has the sheet to itself, like opening a book. The cover comes first, then the title and brief, then the PC with Open the book.

Good

The biggest art, the clearest order, and a real moment before the story starts. It works the same on a phone.

Watch for

Two extra clicks each new game. A Skip link can cover that. It needs a plan for art that's still painting.

B. Cover as the backdrop

Everything on one screen, set over the cover.

The cover fills the whole sheet, darkened so that the words read over it. The title and brief sit on the left, and the PC is inset on the right.

Good

The most cinematic, with no extra clicks. The world's art sets the mood for everything on the page.

Watch for

The cover's own lettered title gets cropped, darkened or doubled, and you lose the cover as a clean image. A story with no cover needs a plain version.

C. One long page

Top to bottom, with the button always in reach.

The pieces run down the middle in your order, like the front pages of a book. Open the book stays pinned at the bottom. Scroll inside the mockups to see it.

Good

The simplest to build and to use, and the same on every screen. The player can skip straight to the story.

Watch for

The first screen shows only the cover, so the title and PC can be missed. It's also the least like a presentation.
